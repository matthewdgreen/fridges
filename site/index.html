04 / Read the fine print

Cold facts.
Clear limitations.

A quiet news cycle is not an all-clear.

What does “No” mean?

This is an editorial assessment of the reported attacks and continuing exposure, not a live test of every fridge. It does not automatically turn to “Yes” after a quiet stretch.

What resets the counter?

A reviewed, substantial attack wave that disrupts refrigeration at multiple sites. We use the incident date, not the date of a later article. Suspected incidents are labeled; a new headline about an old incident does not reset the clock. Days use UTC calendar dates.

What does the Shodan number measure?

The prominent baseline excludes services tagged by Shodan as honeypots; the small-print total includes them. The exposure history retains unfiltered historical totals, while the firmware chart excludes tagged honeypots by default. All counts are matching internet service banners, worldwide, for http.title:"Danfoss StoreView". It is a narrow fingerprint, not all Danfoss products. Multiple ports can inflate the total; one controller can serve many fridges. Shodan's index also lags real-world changes. API methodology ↗

How does this stay up to date?

GitHub Actions requests aggregate Shodan exposure and firmware counts daily at 11:17 UTC and saves their dated history. News is reviewed separately each day by a scheduled Codex task; its last completed review is shown above. Failed collections preserve prior data and show its age. Historical published snapshots remain separate from our own samples.

How can operators reduce exposure?

Follow the manufacturer's and your national CERT's guidance: restrict access to management interfaces, use protected remote access, and apply supported updates. Internet exposure does not by itself identify an exploited vulnerability. Read CERT-FR's advisory ↗
